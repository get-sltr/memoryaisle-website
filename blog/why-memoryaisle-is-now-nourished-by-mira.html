Why we are now Nourished by Mira.

The two questions we hear most on a GLP-1 are “what am I supposed to eat” and “why is my hair falling out.” They turn out to have the same answer. That answer is why we changed our name.

6 min read · Product

MemoryAisle is now Nourished by Mira. Same app, same team, same account. New name.

We changed it because the old one described a grocery aisle, and the thing people actually needed help with was not shopping. It was the part after: standing in a kitchen with no appetite, no hunger signal, and no idea what to eat.

The two questions

Almost every message we get is a version of one of two questions.

The first is what do I eat now. Not in a meal-plan sense. In a literal one. The medication has quieted the signal that used to tell you when and what to eat, and nothing replaced it. Food that used to sound good sounds heavy. You are not being difficult. The mechanism that made eating automatic has gone quiet, and nobody handed you a replacement.

The second is why is my hair coming out in the shower. This one arrives with more fear attached, usually around month three or four, usually after the weight loss has been going well.

For a long time we treated those as two different problems. They are not.

What the hair thing actually is

Hair shedding after significant weight loss is common enough to have a name. Clinicians call it telogen effluvium. In plain terms, a large share of your hair follicles shift out of their growing phase at the same time and into a resting phase, and then shed together a couple of months later. That delay is why it feels like it comes out of nowhere: the trigger was months before the shedding.

The trigger is usually the pace of the loss and the nutritional shortfall that comes with it, not the medication molecule itself. Hair is one of the first things the body deprioritises when protein and micronutrients get thin, because it is not keeping you alive. Your body will not spend on hair before it spends on organs.

Your body will not spend on hair before it spends on organs.

Two things worth saying plainly, because the internet is not careful about either.

First, for most people this is temporary. Telogen effluvium is typically self-limiting, and hair generally recovers once the underlying stressor settles. Second, nobody can promise you that. Hair loss has many causes, some of which have nothing to do with your GLP-1 or your diet, and anyone selling you a supplement with a guarantee attached is selling you something. If it is significant or persistent, it is worth a conversation with your clinician, including bloodwork for the deficiencies that show up in the same pattern.

What is fair to say is this: the same shortfall that thins hair is the one that costs you muscle. Protein and steady, not-too-fast loss are the levers you actually hold, and they are the same levers in both cases.

Which makes it one problem

That is the connection we kept missing, and it is why the name changed.

The scale is going to move on a GLP-1. That part is handled. What is still open is what the lost weight is made of, and that is decided by whether you eat enough of the right thing on the days when eating is hardest. Hair, skin, and muscle are all downstream of the same daily shortfall.

So the job was never to help you shop. The job is to keep you nourished through a period when your body has stopped asking for food.

What Mira does about it

Mira is the assistant inside the app, and she is built around that specific problem rather than around calorie counting.

She knows what you are afraid of. During setup you tell her what worries you most, whether that is hair thinning, losing muscle rather than fat, or your skin. She frames guidance in those terms, because “this protects your hair” lands differently at 9pm on a bad day than “you are 40 grams under target.”

She plans meals around the appetite you actually have. Not the appetite a meal planner assumes you have. Portions, timing, and food choices shift with where you are in your dose cycle, and on days when your stomach is unhappy the gentler options come first.

She shows up on dose day. This is the newest part. If you log your injection, Mira checks in about ninety minutes later, which is when it tends to land. If it is going badly she does not lecture you about your protein target. She offers one small thing: a cup of ginger tea, a few slow sips of a shake, something soft that will actually go down. Then she reframes the day around a protein floor rather than a target you were never going to hit, because on a rough day getting some protein in is the whole job.

She remembers the hard days. Not your conversations, which are never stored. The facts: that you had a dose day, that it was rough. So the next morning opens by acknowledging it instead of pretending it did not happen.

What has not changed

Your account, your history, your logged data, your subscription. All of it carried over. If you had the app as MemoryAisle, it is the same app with a new name on the icon.

What also has not changed is where the line sits. Mira is not a medical provider. She will not tell you a symptom is normal or dangerous, she will not suggest changing your dose, and when something needs a clinician she says so and offers to help you write down what has been happening so you can bring it to that appointment.

The short version

You are on a medication that works. The weight is coming off. The open question is what that weight is made of, and whether you arrive at your goal with your muscle, your hair, and your energy intact.

That comes down to eating enough of the right thing on the days you least feel like eating. Which is a hard thing to do alone, at 9pm, with no appetite and no one asking how you are doing.

That is the job. The name finally says so.
